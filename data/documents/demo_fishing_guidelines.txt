Document: Sustainable Coastal Fisheries & Zonal Regulations
Version: 2026.1-DEMO
Focus: Pelagic Fishing, Mesh Regulations, and Marine Sanctuaries

1. SEASONAL FISHING MORATORIUM (MONSOON BAN)
To promote juvenile stock replenishment and protect spawning pelagic fish (notably Indian Oil Sardine, Mackerel, and Silver Pomfret), an annual 61-day uniform fishing ban is enforced along the East Coast from April 15 to June 14. During this period, all mechanized fishing vessels, including multi-day trawlers and purse-seiners, are prohibited from operating in the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ). Traditional non-motorized craft are exempt from the ban.

2. MINIMUM PERMISSIBLE MESH SIZES
To prevent juvenile fish depletion:
- Trawl cod-end mesh size must not be less than 35 mm (square mesh) or 40 mm (diamond mesh).
- Pelagic gill nets targeted at Sardine and Anchovy must maintain a minimum stretched mesh size of 25 mm.
- Large pelagic drift nets for Seer Fish and Yellowfin Tuna must have a minimum mesh size of 150 mm.
Any catch composed of more than 15% juvenile specimens below the Minimum Legal Size (MLS) is subject to fisheries enforcement penalties.

3. MARINE PROTECTED AREAS & CORAL HABITATS
Fishing is prohibited within 5 nautical miles of designated marine sanctuaries, coral reef reserves, and estuarine nurseries (e.g., Pulicat Lake sanctuary entrance and Gulf of Mannar core zones). Bottom-trawling and dredging in these zones result in automatic vessel impoundment.

4. TARGET SPECIES HABITAT & POTENTIAL FISHING ZONES (PFZ)
Potential Fishing Zones are delineated where Sea Surface Temperature (SST) thermal fronts intersect with elevated Chlorophyll-a concentrations (values exceeding 0.5 to 1.5 mg/m³). In the Bay of Bengal, thermal fronts between 27.5°C and 28.8°C frequently aggregate schools of pelagic carangids, ribbon fish, and skipjack tuna.
